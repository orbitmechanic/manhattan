Weather system
==============

Scope
  viewer/src/weather.rs, assets/shaders/clouds.wesl,
  assets/shaders/precipitation.wesl and the weather inputs in
  viewer/src/environment.rs / viewer/src/main.rs / viewer/src/ui.rs.

Design
  A front-based state machine instead of per-frame procedural noise:

    Clear -> Fair -> Overcast -> Rain -> Thunderstorm

  Winter replaces Rain with Snow in the cycle.  Each front has target cloud
  cover, precipitation, storm strength and wind speed; the current values
  approach those targets with exponential smoothing (roughly 30 s for a full
  transition).  The state machine changes front every 150-320 s when `auto` is
  enabled.  `V` cycles manually; `--weather <front>` and `?weather=<front>`
  start at a fixed front (useful for screenshots and for the web URL).

  Temperature is a New York seasonal/diurnal curve:
    annual = 13 + 13 cos(2 pi (day_of_year - 205) / 365)
    diurnal = 4.5 sin(2 pi (hour - 9) / 24)
  Each front applies a small cooling offset.  When precipitation is active and
  the temperature is below 1.5 C, the precipitation blend becomes snow; the
  explicit "snow" preset forces -1.5 C.

Clouds
  Two camera-following horizontal planes at 1.18 km and 2.05 km, 15 km and
  23 km across.  assets/shaders/clouds.wesl evaluates three octaves of value
  noise in world space.  Uniforms:
    data  = time, cover, wind x, wind z
    lit   = sunlit colour + layer opacity
    shade = shadow colour
    extra = lightning flash, noise scale
  Because the noise is sampled in world space, the clouds stay put as the
  planes follow the camera; only the wind uniform scrolls the pattern.  Cover
  maps to a soft threshold, so a 5% clear sky through 96% storm deck uses the
  same shader.  The planes are double-sided transparent geometry with no depth
  write; from the overview altitude their opacity is reduced to 30% so they
  do not hide the city.

Precipitation
  One open-ended cylinder (radius 65 m, 140 m tall, no caps) follows the
  camera.  assets/shaders/precipitation.wesl generates every streak/flake per
  fragment:
    - rain: thin world-space columns scrolled downwards, slanted by the wind
      and gapped along their fall cycle; two offset samples increase density
    - snow: larger drifting flakes with a short vertical life
  The material disables the prepass, sets depth compare to Always and turns
  depth writes off, so precipitation falls in front of the whole scene
  (including foreground vehicles) without extra draw passes.  The volume is
  hidden above 750 m and when precipitation is below 2%.

  Since the volume is a single transparent surface, it is cheap: one draw call
  and a handful of ALU operations per covered pixel.

Lighting / atmosphere integration
  environment.rs reads Weather and:
    - blends the clear-sky colour toward a grey cloud deck (darker in storms)
    - attenuates the sun by up to ~88% and the moon by cloud cover
    - raises ambient diffuse fill on overcast days
    - adds lightning to sky and ambient for a few frames
    - shrinks fog visibility: base 5 km + 2.2 m per world-unit altitude,
      then (1 - 0.52 cover - 0.30 precip - 0.10 storm), clamped 1.2-22 km
    - hides stars above 55% cloud, the sun disc above 72%, the moon above 55%
  update_dof stops the lens down as precipitation rises (f/1 -> f/17, CoC
  18 -> 0.7 px) so streaks stay legible instead of being blurred away.

Lightning
  A storm front above 42% schedules isolated flashes every 2.4-15 s.  The
  flash decays at 2.8/s and is fed to the sky, ambient and cloud shader.  No
  light entities or shadow maps are spawned.

HUD / controls
  The top-left panel adds:
    Weather <front>  cloud NN%  precip NN%  wind NN km/h  NN.N C
  The bottom-left help adds `WEATHER: V cycles ...`.
  `--weather clear|fair|overcast|rain|storm|snow`
  `?weather=clear|fair|overcast|rain|storm|snow`

Performance
  Native and web use the same path:
    - 2 cloud plane draws (6 triangles total)
    - 1 precipitation cylinder draw (128 triangles, no caps)
    - 2 custom material updates per frame
    - no particle buffers, no per-drop entities, no shadow maps
  Shader cost is three noise octaves on the cloud planes and a few hash calls
  on the precipitation shell.  The cloud planes are skipped only by their own
  transparent pass; they remain below/above the camera and are not culled.
